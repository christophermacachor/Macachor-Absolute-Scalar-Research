Abstract

This paper introduces the Macachor First Derivative as a proposed theoretical framework for extracting structural information from an underlying scalar substrate before that structure is decomposed into conventional measurement, representation, and semantic categories.

The framework distinguishes three epistemic layers: established mathematics, the Macachor formalism (scalar substrate φ and its first derivative φ'), and hypotheses concerning coherence, AI, semantic reconstruction, and quantum-state analogy that require independent validation.

Central Principle

“No semantic claim should be treated as fundamental unless its structural provenance is identified.”

Key Concepts

  • Scalar Substrate (φ): The underlying, unmeasured field of information from which all structure and meaning arises.
  • First Derivative (φ'): The minimal structural translation layer. It expresses how the underlying structure changes across a domain. It is the bridge between the raw substrate and any observable representation.
  • Structural Provenance: The requirement that any semantic claim must be traceable back to its origin in the scalar substrate.
