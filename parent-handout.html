Why Does My Child Feel Unsafe?

The Gut‑Brain Connection — A Guide for Parents

Your child is not “acting out.” Their gut is sending a jittery signal that their brain reads as danger. This handout explains why — and what you can do at home.

1. What Is Really Happening?

Your child’s gut is not just a food tube. It is a 9‑meter‑long sense organ — a “second brain” that beats like a slow, steady drum.

  • Steady drumbeat: brain hears: “I am safe. I can eat, play, explore.”
  • Jittery drumbeat (skipping CD): brain hears: “Something is wrong — I don’t know what, but it’s dangerous.”

When the gut is jittery, your child cannot tell if they are hungry, anxious, full, or sick — so the brain defaults to fight‑or‑flight.

3. What You Can Do at Home — 6 Simple Daily Steps

1. Slow Breathing Together

Breathe in for 4 seconds, out for 6 seconds — 5.5 breaths per minute — for 2 minutes before meals.
Why: Steadies the gut drumbeat — gives the brain a clear “safe” signal.

2. Eat at the Same Time Every Day

Regular meal times, within 30 minutes, every day.
Why: Trains the gut to predict food — reduces surprise jitter.

3. Gentle Pressure Before Meals

A hug, weighted blanket, slow rocking, or deep back rub — for 2 minutes before eating.
Why: Mechanical pressure entrains the gut rhythm — like a calming lullaby.

4. Fermented Foods (Small Amounts)

Yogurt, kefir, sauerkraut, or a probiotic — daily.
Why: Feeds the “good bugs” that help the gut speak clearly to the brain.

5. Pause Between Bites

Put the fork down between bites — slow, rhythmic chewing.
Why: Keeps the gut signal stable during the meal.

6. Name the Feeling

Instead of “Are you hungry?” say: “Is your tummy saying hungry or worried?”
Why: Gives the brain words to match the signal — reduces confusion.

7. When to Contact Your Occupational Therapist

  • If meltdowns persist after 4 weeks of daily practice
  • If your child loses weight or refuses all foods
  • If your child seems in pain or extremely distressed during meals

You are not failing. You are learning a new language — the language of your child’s body.
One step at a time. One meal at a time. One calm breath at a time.
